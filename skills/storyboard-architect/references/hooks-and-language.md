# Hooks and the language pass

The plan is only as good as the words on screen. Do this pass after the shots are
planned and before the files are written. It changes words, not shots.

## The hook is shot one

A viewer decides in the first second. The first line has to give them a reason to stay,
and it has to be true.

| Pattern | Shape | Example |
|---|---|---|
| The moment they know | Name the thing that just went wrong, in their words | "The breaker tripped again." |
| The object | Point at one thing in the frame and say what it is | "That was on your roof." |
| The question they already ask | Their own question, not yours | "Still hot inside?" |
| The news | Something is new, here, now | "The fall roast is here." |
| The number | One figure the brief gave you, with its unit | Only if the brief has one |
| The turn | What they assume, then the opposite | "Older panels were built for less." |

Pick the pattern from what the first picture shows. If the line could run over any other
picture, it is not a hook, it is a slogan.

A hook is not:
- A greeting, a brand name or a welcome.
- A question the viewer would answer "no" to.
- A claim nobody can check ("the best", "number one", "trusted").

## The language pass

Go through every overlay and every voiceover line and ask:

1. **Is it specific?** Replace the category with the thing. "Fast service" becomes "in
   one visit". "Quality ingredients" becomes "dark cocoa, fig, cedar".
2. **Is it one idea?** If there is an "and", it is probably two lines or one too many.
3. **Would the customer say it?** Use their word for the problem, not the trade's.
   "Tripped" not "overloaded circuit".
4. **Does it say what happens?** Prefer a verb and an outcome to an adjective. "Everything
   on. Nothing trips." beats "reliable power".
5. **Is it short enough to read twice?** See the word limits in `on-screen-text.md`.
6. **Is it true and in the brief?** No number, price, guarantee, award or customer the
   brief did not give. If the line needs one, ask or cut the line.
7. **Does the picture already say it?** Then the words should add, not describe. Do not
   caption what is visible.
8. **Does the last line tell them what to do?** One action, one place: "Book a panel
   check online." Not three options.

## Words to cut

They fill space and say nothing. `validate_shots.py` does not check these; you do.

- Empty praise: best, leading, premium, world-class, quality, trusted, amazing.
- Empty verbs: transform, elevate, unlock, empower, reimagine, revolutionize.
- Throat-clearing: introducing, welcome to, meet, discover, we believe, at [brand] we.
- Hedges: may, might, can help, up to, some of the.
- Stacked adjectives. One, or none.

## Across the whole video

- Read the lines top to bottom with the pictures covered. They should make a small
  argument: problem, turn, proof, ask.
- Keep one grammar. If shot two is a sentence, shot five is not a single shouted word.
- The brand name appears once, at the end, as a lockup. It does not need saying twice.
- Mark emphasis on the words a viewer would repeat to someone else.

## When the copy is the user's

If the brief gives exact lines, keep them. Run the pass, and report what you would
change and why in the reply. Do not rewrite a client's approved line on your own.
